01 / Research experience

Selected research

My work spans computational photonics, liquid-crystal photoalignment, and hands-on optical device fabrication.

01Jul. 2026–Present

Anisotropy Control of Dirac Cones in 3D Photonic-Crystal Slabs

Undergraduate Researcher · Prof. Jingwen Ma’s Group, SUSTech ↗

This project examines how material anisotropy governs the formation, migration, tilting, gap opening, and modal coupling of Dirac cones in three-dimensional photonic-crystal slabs.

My contribution

Built FDTD models and Python/LSF automated parameter sweeps for anisotropic dielectric tensors; extracted band structures, located Dirac points, quantified cone tilt, and analyzed two-dimensional momentum-space dispersions and modal fields.

Photonic crystalsFDTD simulationBand-structure analysisDirac cones
02Jul. 2025–Mar. 2026

Liquid-Crystal Photoalignment and Optical Characterization

Undergraduate Researcher · Prof. Dan Luo’s Group, SUSTech ↗

This project explores how patterned photoalignment can control liquid-crystal orientation and optical response, enabling angle-dependent grayscale modulation for multidimensional optical information encoding.

My contribution

Initially explored self-organized helical structures, then systematically varied SD1 concentration, DMD exposure time, and liquid-crystal layer thickness to analyze angle-dependent color, intensity, and transmittance for multidimensional encrypted displays.

SD1 photoalignmentDMD exposurePolarized optical microscopy
01 · DMD exposure time and grayscale

Under DMD-patterned polarized-light exposure, longer exposure produced more ordered SD1 alignment and therefore a larger bright–dark contrast after liquid-crystal coating.

02 · Spin-coated thickness and color

Changing the spin-coated liquid-crystal thickness shifted the observed interference color from yellow through cyan to blue.

03Jul.–Aug. 2025

Fabrication of a Photonic-Crystal Visuotactile Sensor

Selected Research Project · Prof. Dan Luo’s Group, SUSTech ↗

This project develops an ultrathin photonic-crystal visuotactile sensor that converts contact-induced deformation into camera-readable optical information for tactile and force sensing.

My contribution

Participated in the full fabrication process, including layered spin coating on glass, deposition of white tracking markers, sample encapsulation, and integration with a handheld camera-and-illumination setup.
